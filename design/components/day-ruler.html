Day ruler: jump to any day from the side

"On a large screen I'd also like tick marks with dates along the side. They scroll separately from the main screen, so you can quickly jump to a specific day, like the table of contents in Notion. Each tick is a separate day, showing the day of the week. Monday's tick is a little longer than the others, and the first day of a month is longer still." - the owner
"The design is wrong: the scale is too small. There must be no vertical connecting line. Show the time that stands before the slash in the day card, i.e. accounting for overlap. The ticks must be right-aligned, fully flush against the right edge of the screen. On hover a tick should smoothly grow and gain opacity. Look at how Notion does it." - the owner, on revision 2

This page is the design of that ruler on the Timer page, as implemented in DayRuler.svelte and lib/ruler.ts: a live demo in a mock desktop window, its anatomy and states in both themes, placement and breakpoints, the alternatives that lost, and the spec. Revision 2 applied the owner's first review: the hours bar went - the day's time became quiet text; month headers became clickable; weekends and time off are the Reports chart's bands; the ruler is mouse-only. Revision 3 applies the second, with Notion's outline as the model: a larger scale (20px rows, 12px type); no spine and no thumb - 2px ticks flush with the right edge of the window, and the ticks of the days the feed shows lit in the accent; a hovered tick grows and comes up to full strength; the time is the day header's clock time, parallel work counted once. After review the rows went to px (the model's own units), the range is lit at 80% under a full-strength current day, and 85-88rem windows got a compact ruler without the times instead of none. Then, at the user's request, the type went up a step - 13px labels, 12px times, a 14px year row and a 12px chip, the rows unchanged - and the times moved to 89rem to make room; the tooltip grew with the labels, and today's bold weekday now spills left instead of into its date. Last, also at the user's request, the rows went 3px further apart - a 23px pitch - so fewer days fit on screen (35 instead of 41 at 900px high). The feed it drives is the live one (feed.ts + FeedScroller): newest first, virtualised, with reveal(iso) able to put any day under the pinned strip.

Placement and breakpoints

The shell is one width on every page (68rem, cards 66rem), so the ruler cannot borrow space from the column without moving the header between pages. It lives only in the free margin, against the window's right edge; the margin is wide enough for dates and ticks from 85rem, and for the times too from 89rem.

  • Horizontal. right: 0; width: min(11rem, calc(50% - 33.75rem)) of the viewport: flush with the right edge of the window (the left edge of a classic scrollbar), so the ticks sit against it as Notion's outline does. The width is the margin right of the cards less 12px, capped at 11rem (176px): from 1360 to 1432px wide the ruler is exactly 12px clear of the cards, and a wider window only adds air between the cards and the labels - never between the ticks and the edge.
  • Vertical. Fixed, from top: 0.75rem (level with the header's content when the page is at the top) to the bottom of the window. The header is not sticky and scrolls away; the ruler does not follow it, because a ruler whose top moved with the page would push its own bottom rows off-screen at the top of the page. The pinned strip lives inside the column, so the two never meet.
  • Never over content. The ruler itself only ever occupies the margin. The tooltip is the one thing that crosses into the column - it is transient, appears only while the ruler is being pointed at, and never on its own.
  • Compact between 85 and 89rem. @media (width < 89rem) { .dr-dur { display: none } } - a range query, so no fractional width just under 89rem is left with the times in a ruler too narrow for them: the margin holds the weekday, the date and the ticks (about 98px) but not the times, so they drop out of the rows and stay in the tooltip and the accessible name. Nothing else changes - no second layout, no other size.
  • Hidden means not rendered. Below 85rem, or without a fine hovering pointer, the component does not mount: no DOM, no landmark, no tab stop, no scroll listener. A window resized across 85rem mounts or unmounts it (matchMedia listener on (min-width: 85rem) and (hover: hover) and (pointer: fine) in TimerPage.svelte).
  • Mouse only. No touch version for now (owner's decision). A tablet - an iPad Pro in landscape is 1366px wide - has no hovering pointer, so it gets no ruler, whatever its width. A touchscreen laptop with a mouse or trackpad does get it.
  • Only the Timer page. The other pages have no day feed.

Behaviour

  1. Range. From today (the top row, always, entries or not) down to the oldest day with a finished entry, every calendar day in between. Not rounded to the month's start: every row maps to a real place in the feed, and the ruler simply ends at the first recorded day's tick. Future time off is not shown.
  2. What a day row says. Tick length = calendar (day, Monday, 1st). Tick strength = has entries; the labels stay muted either way and brighten only under the pointer or focus. The time = how long by the clock, the day header's first figure. Band = weekend or time off (time off wins). Accent label = today. Lit tick = the feed shows the day; full strength and a glow = the day at the reading line. Nothing else varies. Empty days stay quiet - fainter tick, no time; the band, not the dimming, is what says "weekend".
  3. Click or Enter jumps the feed, instantly. A day with entries: scroller.reveal(iso) - its top under the pinned strip. A day without: reveal of the nearest older day with entries - the place the day would occupy in a newest-first feed. Today, and any empty day newer than the newest recorded day: the top of the page (scrollTo(0)), where the start form and running timers are. A month header: the month's newest day with entries - the top of the page when that is today, as for today's row; a month without entries lands where the month would be (its oldest day's target). Then the landing ring.
  4. Why not smooth. A distant jump would stream through unmounted months, and every FeedScroller anchor correction is an instant scroll that cancels a smooth one. The ring does the orientation work a smooth scroll would have done. (Try "Smooth" in the demo: fine in a mock with everything mounted, not in the real feed.)
  5. The lit ticks follow the feed continuously. The reading line (the strip's bottom when stuck, else the window top) and the window bottom are each mapped into the ruler (rulerOffset): inside a day's card at fraction f → that row's top + f × 23; inside the gap after it → linearly across the empty rows (and any header) down to the next recorded row; above the feed → today's row. The rows that span overlaps are the visible range, and their ticks light up; so the lit run moves with the page and crosses a weekend as the feed crosses the gap between Friday and Monday. The day at the reading line is aria-current, and its tick is lit above the rest - full strength and a glow. Nothing draws the span itself: it only steers the ruler's scrolling and the chip.
  6. Following. After each page scroll, if the lit span has left the comfort band - from the stuck headers + 15% of the rest to 80% of the ruler's height - the ruler scrolls the minimum that brings it back (the span's top wins if it is taller than the band). Always instantly: during a scroll the ruler moves in lock-step with the page, and after a big page jump (Home, End, a scrollbar drag, the first scroll after exploring the ruler) it cuts to the new place once - a long smooth scroll of dates in the corner of the eye pulls attention away from the feed.
  7. Never fights the user. Following is suspended while the pointer is over the ruler and while it holds keyboard focus (:focus-visible). A mouse click takes no focus at all, as in Safari, so the page keys go on scrolling the page - unless keyboard focus is already in the ruler, where it moves to the clicked row. A wheel over the ruler scrolls only the ruler (overscroll-behavior: contain). A jump started from the ruler never makes it scroll.
  8. Resuming. Following resumes on the first page scroll after the pointer and keyboard focus have both left. Until then the ruler stays wherever the reader left it; if the lit ticks are out of view there, the return chip says where they are and scrolls the ruler back on click.
  9. Hover. No hover band on a day: its labels go bright at once, and its tick reaches out 10px and comes up to full strength over 180ms - and back when the pointer moves on, so a sweep down the ruler leaves a short wave behind it. The tooltip appears after 250ms of hover intent, then follows the pointer from row to row with no delay; it hides 120ms after the pointer leaves the ruler, and the next entry needs the delay again. A month header's name takes the lit colour, and it shows a tooltip with the jump target; year rows are inert.
  10. Live data. The model is rebuilt from feedDays and appState.timeOff whenever they change (one pass, keyed by day, so only changed rows re-render). Today's time counts finished entries only - the same figure as today's card - so it changes when a timer stops, never per second or per minute. A new day at midnight adds a row at the top (and a header on the 1st) without moving what the reader is looking at: the ruler keeps its scroll offset from the bottom when it is not at the top.
  11. Mounting. Only when the feed has at least one finished day before today; below that there is nothing to navigate. Only with a mouse (behaviour of the media query, not a setting).

Keyboard and accessibility

  1. Landmark. <nav aria-label={t("Days")}> placed in the DOM after the running timers and before the feed: navigation, then content. One tab stop (roving tabindex) across month headers and days.
  2. Structure. Per month: the header button, then one <ol aria-label="September 2026"> of its days ("September 2026, list, 30 items"). The list is named directly, not by the header, because the header's name is now an action. Year rows, ticks (lit or not - aria-current says where the reader is), times (they repeat the name) and bands are presentational.
  3. Names. Each day is a <button> named with the full localised date and its state, the time being the clock time - and, where parallel work sets it apart, the tracked sum after it: "Tuesday, September 22, 2026, 7h 35m, 5 entries", "…, 1h 30m, 2h 10m tracked - work that ran in parallel is counted once, 3 entries", "Saturday, September 19, 2026, no entries", "…, vacation". Today starts with "Today,". The visible text Tue 22 7h 35m is an abbreviation of that name - a deliberate trade against WCAG 2.5.3 in favour of a complete name. A month header is "September 2026, go to the newest day with entries" (its visible text first, so 2.5.3 holds), or "July 2026, no entries, jumps to Tuesday, June 30, 2026".
  4. aria-current="location" on the day at the reading line - the tick at full strength, glowing; it moves as the page scrolls. While focus is outside the ruler, the roving tab stop follows it too, so Tab lands on "where I am".
  5. Keys. ↑/↓ previous/next stop in visual order - every month header and every calendar day; PageUp/PageDown the same date one month newer/older (clamped), or from a header the neighbouring month's header; Shift+PageUp/PageDown one year; Home today; End the oldest day; Enter/Space jump; Escape hides the tooltip. The ruler scrolls to keep focus clear of its stuck headers. Its key handler calls preventDefault, so FeedScroller's page-key handler (which checks defaultPrevented) leaves the page alone.
  6. Focus after a jump stays on the ruler, so the reader can keep browsing. The next Tab moves into the landed day - its first control - the way an in-page link moves the sequential focus starting point. Shift+Tab leaves the ruler backwards as usual.
  7. Tooltip also shows for keyboard focus (:focus-visible), is dismissible (Escape) and hoverable-safe (it never sits under the pointer: pointer-events: none, to the left of the ruler). It duplicates the button's name, tracked sum included, so it is aria-hidden.
  8. Contrast. On the background / on the palest band: labels and times 6.2 / 4.8 dark, 5.3 / 4.6 light; bright dates 13.4 / 10.2 dark, 13.1 / 11.3 light; today 8.1 / 6.2 dark, 5.3 / 4.5 light; ticks on the background, dark / light: the current day 8.1 / 4.9, the rest of the lit range (0.8) 5.6 / 3.3, an unlit day with entries (0.45) 3.7 / 2.6, an empty day (0.22) 1.8 / 1.5 - so in both themes the lit range reads above the unlit ticks and below the current one. The sick hatch is measured on its fill; its 0.8px stripes are texture. Ticks and bands repeat what the labels and names say, so they are decorative. The focus ring is the system's --accent ring (2.6:1 on the light background - the system-wide token issue already noted in the pinned strip spec).
  9. Reduced motion (prefers-reduced-motion: reduce): a hovered or focused tick grows at once (no transition), the return chip scrolls the ruler instantly (the only smooth scroll the ruler has), the landing ring is static for 1.2s instead of fading. The page jump and following are instant anyway; the tooltip has no animation.
  10. Forced colours. Opacity and the glow are the ordinary marks, so here every tick is solid (opacity 1, the lit ones included): ticks CanvasText, lit ticks Highlight, and the day at the reading line reaches out instead - width: calc(var(--dr-len) + var(--dr-tick-grow)), as long as a hovered one. Time off a 3px CanvasText edge instead of the band; weekend bands dropped (the weekday says it).

Engineering notes

  • Reader-input detection. FeedScroller treats a wheel or scroll key anywhere as the reader scrolling the page (it holds probes and the running card for 250ms). Events whose target is inside the ruler must be ignored there: the ruler scrolling is not the page scrolling.
  • Page keys. With focus on a ruler button, PageDown must page the ruler, not the feed. The ruler's own handler runs first (on the nav) and calls preventDefault; #onKeydown already returns on defaultPrevented. Arrow keys too - otherwise the browser scrolls the ruler's scroller natively on top of the roving focus.
  • Landing ring is a class on the day wrapper after reveal resolves (the day is mounted by then); removed on animationend or after 1.2s.
  • Tab into the landed day: remember { button, iso } of the last jump; on Tab (no Shift) from that same button, preventDefault and focus the first control in .day[data-key=iso]. Cleared on any other focus change.
  • Weekday column width = the widest of the 7 short weekday names in the UI locale, measured once per locale with canvas.measureText in the ruler's font, set as --dr-wd-w. With Segoe UI or Roboto the widths differ from macOS; measuring keeps the dates in one column everywhere.
  • Midnight - todayISO already derives from clock.now once a day; the model rebuild adds the new row. Keep the ruler's scrollTop anchored from the bottom when it is not at 0 so the rows under the reader do not shift by 23px.
  • content-visibility is auto on every month but the two newest (.near), which are what the ruler shows when it mounts - so the first frame can never be blank. In Chromium, a far jump of the ruler's own scroller (End, a resync) renders the newly visible months in the same frame (checked here).
  • No ResizeObserver, no measuring in the ruler: pitch and header heights are constants, the lit span and every row's top are arithmetic. The only DOM reads are the ruler's own scrollTop/clientHeight and the tooltip's size.
  • Lit ticks are marks, not an element. The component sets data-vis and aria-current by hand on the rows that change (a few per frame of scrolling), and clears them when the model is rebuilt; toggling a class on thousands of rows reactively would rerun every row each frame. The tick's colour, opacity (0.8 for the range, 1 for the current day) and glow are CSS on those attributes, so there is no thumb to position and nothing a month header can cut.
  • Rows in px, type in rem. The model's offsets - RULER_PITCH, RULER_HEAD, every row's top - are px, so --dr-pitch and --dr-head are px too. In rem, a reader's 20px default font made every row 25px: the rows drifted away from the offsets the lit span and following are computed in, and following lost the day at the reading line. The e2e suite pins it: at a 20px default font a row is 23px and a header 28px.
  • Clock time comes from the same helper as the card header's first figure - formatDurationShort(wallClockMs(day.entries, 0)) - computed once per day in the model, not per render; trackedMs sits beside it for the tooltip and the name, which add {tracked} tracked - work that ran in parallel is counted once only when the two differ. The width budget is the widest locale: 6 (labels start) + 29 (fr weekday at 13px, 28.8 rounded up as the column is) + 3 + 16.4 (date, 2ch at 13px) + 8 + 50.6 (23h 59m, 7 characters of 12px mono) + 44 (the tick zone) = 157px by its parts, measured here in Chromium. Rendered, with two years of data in every locale, the widest row (fr) takes 157.9px in Chromium and 159.1 in WebKit, whose mono is wider (51.9). The ruler gets at least 163.5px from 89rem (with a classic 17px scrollbar - 5.6px to spare in Chromium, 4.4 in WebKit; 164.5 beside a 15px one, 172 with overlay scrollbars), 176 from 1432px. 88rem would give 155.5px beside a classic scrollbar - 2.4px short in Chromium, 3.6 in WebKit - so the times moved to 89rem with the type. Below 89rem the time and its gap go - 98.4px (98.2 in WebKit) - and the ruler gets at least 131.5px (85rem, classic scrollbar), 33px to spare. Seven characters hold anything up to 99h 59m; a day of 100h or more (a timer left running for days) takes eight, which uses about 1px of the 6px tick clearance beside a classic scrollbar at 89rem - invisible. With parallel work counted once a day's clock time only passes 24h when an entry runs on past midnight - the tracked sum, which could (31h 05m of parallel agents), is no longer what the ruler shows.
  • Bands share their values with DailyChart: move BAND_COLORS, the weekend colour and the hatch into one module (or tokens) when this lands, so the two can never drift. The chart's hatch is an SVG pattern; the ruler's is the CSS gradient above, checked side by side against it.
  • Strings (new). t("Days") (nav), t("No entries"), t("jumps to {day}"), t("jumps to the top"), t("Today"), t("{month}, go to the newest day with entries"), t("{month}, no entries, {jump}"). t("{tracked} tracked - work that ran in parallel is counted once") (tooltip and name; the day header's longer t("{wall} on the clock, {tracked} tracked - …") is not reused, since the clock time is already on the line above). Reused: t("{n} entries"), t("vacation"), t("sick leave"), t("day off"). Dates, weekdays and September 2026 come from Intl with formattingLocale().

Decisions and alternatives

  • Right side, not left. The right pairs with the scrollbar, the reader's existing "where am I"; the day totals and durations are right-aligned, so the eye already travels there; and the left margin starts every line of reading. Left would sit next to the day headings - the only thing it had going for it.
  • Notion's outline as the model (owner). Measured on a live Notion page: 2px rounded dashes, 16px long (12px a level deeper), right-aligned, 14px apart; inactive ones the text colour at about 30% (dark rgba(255,252,235,0.306)), the active one bright with box-shadow: 0 0 3px in its own colour; 200ms transitions; hovering the column opens a panel of headings. Taken: 2px rounded bars flush right, --text at an opacity, one dash lit above the rest - the current day at full strength with a 4px glow, the rest of the range the accent at 80% - 180ms transitions, a tick that grows on hover. Not taken: the lengths (ours are the calendar: 12 / 20 / 28px), 14px apart (too tight for a label on every row - 23px), and the panel - our labels are always there, as the owner first asked, so hover only brightens a row and the tooltip gives the full date.
  • No spine, no thumb (owner). The 1px spine joined the ticks into a comb and read as one more line beside the scrollbar; the 3px accent thumb over it said where the feed was, but only on that line. Now the ticks stand alone against the window's edge, and the ticks of the shown days carry the accent themselves - the same information, one mark fewer. Today's tick lost its accent with it: an accent tick means "on screen".
  • Flush with the window's edge (owner). right: 0, not a fixed offset from the cards: the ticks are where the eye finds the scrollbar, and a wider window adds air between the cards and the labels, never between the ticks and the edge. The width follows the margin (at most 176px), so the ruler still never comes closer than 12px to the cards.
  • Compact between 85 and 89rem, not gone. The larger scale and the tick zone need about 159px with the times, which only 89rem gives (88rem did, before the type went up a step); rather than lose the ruler on 1360-1423px windows (a 1366px laptop among them), those get it without the times - dates and ticks need about 98px - and the tooltip and the name carry the time. One CSS rule, not a second layout. Rejected: moving the whole breakpoint to where the times fit (the previous state of this revision).
  • Free margin only, hidden below 85rem. Rejected: shifting the column left to make room (the header would move between pages), overlaying the feed's edge (covers durations), a collapsed rail that expands on hover (hides the very labels the owner asked for).
  • Mouse only (owner). No touch variant for now; the media query requires a hovering fine pointer, so tablets of any width get no ruler. Rejected for now: a drag scrubber with 28px rows and a floating date bubble.
  • Fixed to the viewport, not sticky in the flow below the header (its top would move and its bottom would start off-screen).
  • Every day labelled at a 23px pitch, 13px type (owner: "the scale is too small"; the user: a little more room between rows). Revision 2's 16px and 11px showed more days (52 instead of 35 at 900px) but were too small to read and to hit; revision 3 first went to 20px rows and 12px type. At the user's request the type then went up a step, with the rows unchanged, and after that the rows went 3px further apart - 23px, 35 days at 900px instead of 41 - for air between the 13px labels. Mondays-only labels would be calmer, but the owner asked for every tick to carry its weekday - calm comes from colour instead: dim weekdays, bright dates only where there is work.
  • Month headers above their days, a year row for past years. In a newest-first ruler the 1st is the last row of its month, so the long tick closes the month and the header opens the next one down. A sticky year row keeps every past month unambiguous without crowding the header.
  • Month headers are buttons (owner). A click lands on the month's newest day with entries - the mouse's PageUp/PageDown, and a stuck header becomes "back to the start of this month". Hover band, focus ring and a tooltip with the target, like a day. The header button is a stop in the arrow-key sequence, so the keyboard reaches it the same way. The year row stays inert for now.
  • Empty days are clickable. The ruler is a time axis: any point on it is a place in the feed. Rejected: inert empty rows (dead zones on every weekend; a near-miss does nothing).
  • Ends at the oldest recorded day. Rejected: rounding down to the month's 1st (up to 30 rows of pre-history that go nowhere; drawn in Alternatives).
  • The day's time as quiet text, not a bar (owner). The day header's own figure, so the ruler and the card can never disagree; muted mono, right-aligned so the column reads like the feed's totals, never accented, absent on empty days. Rejected: the hours bar (revision 1 - a shape to compare, but no number), tick opacity by the amount (the opacity now says only whether there are entries at all), no signal.
  • The clock time, not the tracked sum (owner). The figure before the slash in ⏱ 2h 30m / 4h 30m: wallClockMs, parallel work counted once - how long the day took, which is what a reader scanning for a long day means. On a day without parallel work the two are one figure. The tooltip names both, in the header's own sentence, whenever they differ. Revision 2 showed the sum (dayTotal), which parallel agents could push past a working day.
  • Weekends as the Reports chart's band (owner). Same blue band, same time off colours and hatch, same precedence as DailyChart, in both themes - a weekend or a vacation looks the same in the ruler and in the chart. Consecutive weekend days form one continuous block (square ends, no gap). Weekends stay full height: the calendar keeps its true distances. Rejected: half-height weekends (≈25% more days on screen), the previous proposal's rounded time-off runs and theme-token tints.
  • Instant jump + landing ring, not smooth scrolling (see behaviour 4).
  • Plain DOM with content-visibility, not a windowed list or canvas.

Open questions for the owner

  • Today's running time. Today's time counts finished entries only, like today's card; the running timers are in the Running card and the strip. Add them to today's row (refreshed once a minute)?
  • Clickable year row. Jump to the year's newest day with entries, as a month header does? Consistent, but a stuck year row sits 28px above a stuck month header - two targets that close would need to look different.
  • The sick hatch's stripe width. The chart's source says 1.6px, but its 7px pattern tile clips half of the stroke, so the chart shows 0.8px. The ruler matches what the chart shows. If 1.6px is what was meant, fix the chart (offset the line by 0.8px) and change the ruler with it.
