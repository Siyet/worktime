Day ruler: jump to any day from the side

"On a large screen I'd also like tick marks with dates along the side. They scroll separately from the main screen, so you can quickly jump to a specific day, like the table of contents in Notion. Each tick is a separate day, showing the day of the week. Monday's tick is a little longer than the others, and the first day of a month is longer still." - the owner

This page is the proposed design for that ruler on the Timer page: a live demo in a mock desktop window, its anatomy and states in both themes, placement and breakpoints, the alternatives that lost, and the spec. Revision 2 applies the owner's review: the hours bar is gone - the day's tracked time is quiet text instead; month headers are clickable; weekends and time off are the Reports chart's bands; the ruler is mouse-only. The feed it drives is the one that is live today (feed.ts + FeedScroller): newest first, virtualised, with reveal(iso) already able to put any day under the pinned strip.

Placement and breakpoints

The shell is one width on every page (68rem, cards 66rem), so the ruler cannot borrow space from the column without moving the header between pages. It lives only in the free margin, which is wide enough from 85rem.

  • Horizontal. left: calc(50% + 33.75rem) of the viewport: half the 66rem of cards plus 12px, so it tracks the column exactly, whatever the window width. The spine is 16px from the cards - the same gutter as the shell's own padding.
  • Vertical. Fixed, from top: 0.75rem (level with the header's content when the page is at the top) to the bottom of the window. The header is not sticky and scrolls away; the ruler does not follow it, because a ruler whose top moved with the page would push its own bottom rows off-screen at the top of the page. The pinned strip lives inside the column, so the two never meet.
  • Never over content. The ruler itself only ever occupies the margin. The tooltip is the one thing that crosses into the column - it is transient, appears only while the ruler is being pointed at, and never on its own.
  • Hidden means not rendered. Below 85rem, or without a fine hovering pointer, the component does not mount: no DOM, no landmark, no tab stop, no scroll listener. A window resized across 85rem mounts or unmounts it (matchMedia listener).
  • Mouse only. No touch version for now (owner's decision). A tablet - an iPad Pro in landscape is 1366px wide - has no hovering pointer, so it gets no ruler, whatever its width. A touchscreen laptop with a mouse or trackpad does get it.
  • Only the Timer page. The other pages have no day feed.

Behaviour

  1. Range. From today (the top row, always, entries or not) down to the oldest day with a finished entry, every calendar day in between. Not rounded to the month's start: every row maps to a real place in the feed, and the spine simply ends at the first recorded day. Future time off is not shown.
  2. What a day row says. Tick length = calendar (day, Monday, 1st). Date brightness = has entries. The time = how much, in the day header's own words. Band = weekend or time off (time off wins). Accent = today. Nothing else varies. Empty days stay quiet - fainter tick, dim date, no time; the band, not the dimming, is what says "weekend".
  3. Click or Enter jumps the feed, instantly. A day with entries: scroller.reveal(iso) - its top under the pinned strip. A day without: reveal of the nearest older day with entries - the place the day would occupy in a newest-first feed. Today, and any empty day newer than the newest recorded day: the top of the page (scrollTo(0)), where the start form and running timers are. A month header: the month's newest day with entries - the top of the page when that is today, as for today's row; a month without entries lands where the month would be (its oldest day's target). Then the landing ring.
  4. Why not smooth. A distant jump would stream through unmounted months, and every FeedScroller anchor correction is an instant scroll that cancels a smooth one. The ring does the orientation work a smooth scroll would have done. (Try "Smooth" in the demo: fine in a mock with everything mounted, not in the real feed.)
  5. The thumb maps the feed continuously. The reading line (the strip's bottom when stuck, else the window top) and the window bottom are each mapped into the ruler: inside a day's card at fraction f → that row's top + f × 16; inside the gap after it → linearly across the empty rows (and any header) down to the next recorded row; above the feed → today's row. So the thumb glides with the page and crosses a weekend as the feed crosses the gap between Friday and Monday. Rows it overlaps are the visible range; the day at the reading line is aria-current.
  6. Following. After each page scroll, if the thumb has left the comfort band - from the stuck headers + 15% of the rest to 80% of the ruler's height - the ruler scrolls the minimum that brings it back (the thumb's top wins if it is taller than the band). Always instantly: during a scroll the ruler moves in lock-step with the page, and after a big page jump (Home, End, a scrollbar drag, the first scroll after exploring the ruler) it cuts to the new place once - a long smooth scroll of dates in the corner of the eye pulls attention away from the feed.
  7. Never fights the user. Following is suspended while the pointer is over the ruler and while it holds keyboard focus (:focus-visible). A mouse click takes no focus at all, as in Safari, so the page keys go on scrolling the page - unless keyboard focus is already in the ruler, where it moves to the clicked row. A wheel over the ruler scrolls only the ruler (overscroll-behavior: contain). A jump started from the ruler never makes it scroll.
  8. Resuming. Following resumes on the first page scroll after the pointer and keyboard focus have both left. Until then the ruler stays wherever the reader left it; if the thumb is out of view there, the return chip says where it is and scrolls the ruler back on click.
  9. Hover. The row gets the hover band and bright labels at once; the tooltip appears after 250ms of hover intent, then follows the pointer from row to row with no delay; it hides 120ms after the pointer leaves the ruler, and the next entry needs the delay again. Month headers behave the same (hover band, tooltip with the jump target); year rows are inert.
  10. Live data. The model is rebuilt from feedDays and appState.timeOff whenever they change (one pass, keyed by day, so only changed rows re-render). Today's time counts finished entries only - the same figure as today's card - so it changes when a timer stops, never per second or per minute. A new day at midnight adds a row at the top (and a header on the 1st) without moving what the reader is looking at: the ruler keeps its scroll offset from the bottom when it is not at the top.
  11. Mounting. Only when the feed has at least one finished day before today; below that there is nothing to navigate. Only with a mouse (behaviour of the media query, not a setting).

Keyboard and accessibility

  1. Landmark. <nav aria-label={t("Days")}> placed in the DOM after the running timers and before the feed: navigation, then content. One tab stop (roving tabindex) across month headers and days.
  2. Structure. Per month: the header button, then one <ol aria-label="September 2026"> of its days ("September 2026, list, 30 items"). The list is named directly, not by the header, because the header's name is now an action. Year rows, ticks, times (they repeat the name), bands and the thumb are presentational.
  3. Names. Each day is a <button> named with the full localised date and its state: "Tuesday, September 22, 2026, 7h 35m, 5 entries", "Saturday, September 19, 2026, no entries", "…, vacation". Today starts with "Today,". The visible text Tue 22 7h 35m is an abbreviation of that name - a deliberate trade against WCAG 2.5.3 in favour of a complete name. A month header is "September 2026, go to the newest day with entries" (its visible text first, so 2.5.3 holds), or "July 2026, no entries, jumps to Tuesday, June 30, 2026".
  4. aria-current="location" on the day at the reading line; it moves as the page scrolls. While focus is outside the ruler, the roving tab stop follows it too, so Tab lands on "where I am".
  5. Keys. ↑/↓ previous/next stop in visual order - every month header and every calendar day; PageUp/PageDown the same date one month newer/older (clamped), or from a header the neighbouring month's header; Shift+PageUp/PageDown one year; Home today; End the oldest day; Enter/Space jump; Escape hides the tooltip. The ruler scrolls to keep focus clear of its stuck headers. Its key handler calls preventDefault, so FeedScroller's page-key handler (which checks defaultPrevented) leaves the page alone.
  6. Focus after a jump stays on the ruler, so the reader can keep browsing. The next Tab moves into the landed day - its first control - the way an in-page link moves the sequential focus starting point. Shift+Tab leaves the ruler backwards as usual.
  7. Tooltip also shows for keyboard focus (:focus-visible), is dismissible (Escape) and hoverable-safe (it never sits under the pointer: pointer-events: none, to the left of the ruler). It duplicates the button's name, so it is aria-hidden.
  8. Contrast. On the background / on the palest band: labels and times 6.2 / 4.8 dark, 5.3 / 4.6 light; bright dates 13.4 / 10.2 dark, 13.1 / 11.3 light; today 8.1 / 6.2 dark, 5.3 / 4.5 light; thumb 8.1 / 3.8 (on the background). The sick hatch is measured on its fill; its 0.8px stripes are texture. Ticks and bands repeat what the labels and names say, so they are decorative. The focus ring is the system's --accent ring (2.6:1 on the light background - the system-wide token issue already noted in the pinned strip spec).
  9. Reduced motion (prefers-reduced-motion: reduce): the return chip scrolls the ruler instantly (the only smooth scroll the ruler has), the landing ring is static for 1.2s instead of fading. The page jump and following are instant anyway; the tooltip has no animation.
  10. Forced colours. Spine and ticks CanvasText; thumb Highlight; time off a 3px CanvasText edge instead of the band; weekend bands dropped (the weekday says it).

Engineering notes

  • Reader-input detection. FeedScroller treats a wheel or scroll key anywhere as the reader scrolling the page (it holds probes and the running card for 250ms). Events whose target is inside the ruler must be ignored there: the ruler scrolling is not the page scrolling.
  • Page keys. With focus on a ruler button, PageDown must page the ruler, not the feed. The ruler's own handler runs first (on the nav) and calls preventDefault; #onKeydown already returns on defaultPrevented. Arrow keys too - otherwise the browser scrolls the ruler's scroller natively on top of the roving focus.
  • Landing ring is a class on the day wrapper after reveal resolves (the day is mounted by then); removed on animationend or after 1.2s.
  • Tab into the landed day: remember { button, iso } of the last jump; on Tab (no Shift) from that same button, preventDefault and focus the first control in .day[data-key=iso]. Cleared on any other focus change.
  • Weekday column width = the widest of the 7 short weekday names in the UI locale, measured once per locale with canvas.measureText in the ruler's font, set as --dr-wd-w. With Segoe UI or Roboto the widths differ from macOS; measuring keeps the dates in one column everywhere.
  • Midnight - todayISO already derives from clock.now once a day; the model rebuild adds the new row. Keep the ruler's scrollTop anchored from the bottom when it is not at 0 so the rows under the reader do not shift by 16px.
  • content-visibility is auto on every month but the two newest (.near), which are what the ruler shows when it mounts - so the first frame can never be blank. In Chromium, a far jump of the ruler's own scroller (End, a resync) renders the newly visible months in the same frame (checked here).
  • No ResizeObserver, no measuring in the ruler: pitch and header heights are constants, the thumb and every row's top are arithmetic. The only DOM reads are the ruler's own scrollTop/clientHeight and the tooltip's size.
  • Tracked time comes from the same helpers as the card header - formatDurationShort(dayTotal(groupDayEntries(day.entries))) - computed once per day in the model, not per render. The width budget is the widest locale: 26 (labels start) + 25 (fr weekday) + 3 + 13.8 (date) + 8 + 44.3 (10h 12m, 7 characters of 10.5px mono) = 120.1, + 4px right pad = 124.1, rounded up to 126 to leave room for other platforms' fonts (measured here on two years of data: at least 9px between date and time in every locale, fr the tightest). Parallel agent work can pass 24h (31h 05m still fits); 100h+ would need five timers running all day, and is clipped.
  • Bands share their values with DailyChart: move BAND_COLORS, the weekend colour and the hatch into one module (or tokens) when this lands, so the two can never drift. The chart's hatch is an SVG pattern; the ruler's is the CSS gradient above, checked side by side against it.
  • Strings (new). t("Days") (nav), t("No entries"), t("jumps to {day}"), t("jumps to the top"), t("Today"), t("{month}, go to the newest day with entries"), t("{month}, no entries, {jump}"). Reused: t("{n} entries"), t("vacation"), t("sick leave"), t("day off"). Dates, weekdays and September 2026 come from Intl with formattingLocale().

Decisions and alternatives

  • Right side, not left. The right pairs with the scrollbar, the reader's existing "where am I"; the day totals and durations are right-aligned, so the eye already travels there; and the left margin starts every line of reading. Left would sit next to the day headings - the only thing it had going for it.
  • Free margin only, hidden below 85rem. Rejected: shifting the column left to make room (the header would move between pages), overlaying the feed's edge (covers durations), a collapsed rail that expands on hover (hides the very labels the owner asked for).
  • Mouse only (owner). No touch variant for now; the media query requires a hovering fine pointer, so tablets of any width get no ruler. Rejected for now: a drag scrubber with 28px rows and a floating date bubble.
  • Fixed to the viewport, not sticky in the flow below the header (its top would move and its bottom would start off-screen).
  • Every day labelled at a 16px pitch, 11px type. 14px leaves 3px between lines and reads as a texture; 18px shows 12% fewer days (46 instead of 52 at 900px). Mondays-only labels would be calmer, but the owner asked for every tick to carry its weekday - calm comes from colour instead: dim weekdays, bright dates only where there is work.
  • Month headers above their days, a year row for past years. In a newest-first ruler the 1st is the last row of its month, so the long tick closes the month and the header opens the next one down. A sticky year row keeps every past month unambiguous without crowding the header.
  • Month headers are buttons (owner). A click lands on the month's newest day with entries - the mouse's PageUp/PageDown, and a stuck header becomes "back to the start of this month". Hover band, focus ring and a tooltip with the target, like a day. The header button is a stop in the arrow-key sequence, so the keyboard reaches it the same way. The year row stays inert for now.
  • Empty days are clickable. The ruler is a time axis: any point on it is a place in the feed. Rejected: inert empty rows (dead zones on every weekend; a near-miss does nothing).
  • Ends at the oldest recorded day. Rejected: rounding down to the month's 1st (up to 30 rows of pre-history that go nowhere; drawn in Alternatives).
  • The day's time as quiet text, not a bar (owner). The day header's own figure, formatDurationShort(tracked), so the ruler and the card can never disagree; muted mono, right-aligned so the column reads like the feed's totals, never accented, absent on empty days. It costs 38px (88 → 126px), which moves the breakpoint from 80rem to 85rem: 1280-1359px windows lose the ruler. Rejected: the hours bar (the previous proposal - a shape to compare, but no number), tick opacity, no signal.
  • Weekends as the Reports chart's band (owner). Same blue band, same time off colours and hatch, same precedence as DailyChart, in both themes - a weekend or a vacation looks the same in the ruler and in the chart. Consecutive weekend days form one continuous block (square ends, no gap). Weekends stay full height: the calendar keeps its true distances. Rejected: half-height weekends (≈25% more days on screen), the previous proposal's rounded time-off runs and theme-token tints.
  • Instant jump + landing ring, not smooth scrolling (see behaviour 4).
  • Plain DOM with content-visibility, not a windowed list or canvas.

Open questions for the owner

  • 1280-1359px windows. The wider ruler no longer fits a 1280px window. Accept that, or add a compact 88px variant there (labels only, the time in the tooltip)? It would be a second layout to maintain for one band of window widths.
  • Today's running time. Today's time counts finished entries only, like today's card; the running timers are in the Running card and the strip. Add them to today's row (refreshed once a minute)?
  • Clickable year row. Jump to the year's newest day with entries, as a month header does? Consistent, but a stuck year row sits 24px above a stuck month header - two targets that close would need to look different.
  • The sick hatch's stripe width. The chart's source says 1.6px, but its 7px pattern tile clips half of the stroke, so the chart shows 0.8px. The ruler matches what the chart shows. If 1.6px is what was meant, fix the chart (offset the line by 0.8px) and change the ruler with it.
